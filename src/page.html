Treatment and confidence

Takeaways and decisions

Three to five. End a line with | High, | Moderate, or | Low to show your confidence in it.

What you need the reader to decide or answer. Numbered D1, D2.

What the reader must keep in mind: sensitivities, legal or safety risks, places where accuracy is easy to lose, audience expectations, timing.

The label prints in the margin: Sensitivity, Legal, Accuracy, Audience, Timing, or your own.

How to write the research

Where accounts, experts, or sources disagree, and what is still unknown.

Reference material the reader can look things up in.

Dates such as March 3, 2022 keep their own comma.

Charts, tables, timelines, key numbers, and images. Give each one a short name, then place it in the research or an appendix with [[name]] on a line of its own. Figures and tables are numbered in the order they appear.

Open the list files you saved in the OSINT Citation Generator. Each source gets a short key: write [@key] or [@key, p. 45] in any text. Here you also grade each source, say what it is good for, and choose where it is listed in the source assessment.

Use *asterisks* for italics. A page number in [@key, p. 4] goes before the final period.

No sources yet. You can still write notes by hand with ^[note text].

Grades: a letter for the source (A highly reliable to F unknown) and a number for the information (1 confirmed to 6 unknown), as in the OSINT Style Guide. For sources from a list file, the grade is the one in the citation.

Each # heading starts Appendix A, B, C. ## headings are numbered A.1, A.2. [[rating-key]] adds the full source rating key table.
